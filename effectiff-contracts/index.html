How supplier registration works
  1. Find the supplier portal. Look for "Suppliers," "Supplier diversity," or "Doing business with us" on the company's site. Many run on SAP Ariba, Coupa, or Jaggaer. Research company finds it for you.
  2. Check whether registration is open. At many large companies, a buyer inside the company has to sponsor you before you can finish onboarding. Open registration usually adds you to a database buyers can search.
  3. Prepare the standard documents.
    • W-9
    • Certificate of insurance (general liability and professional liability are common; cyber liability is increasingly requested)
    • Business license
    • A one-page capability statement: languages, service types, turnaround times, notable clients
    • References and a rate sheet
    • A D-U-N-S number, which many companies still ask for
  4. Get certified if you qualify. Supplier diversity programs recognize certifications such as NMSDC (minority-owned), WBENC (women-owned), NGLCC (LGBTQ+-owned), Disability:IN (disability-owned), and NaVOBA or SBA VetCert (veteran-owned). In California, the CPUC Supplier Clearinghouse verifies diverse suppliers for utilities. For language services, ISO 17100 (translation) and ISO 18841 (interpreting) carry weight, and healthcare clients will ask how you handle HIPAA.
  5. Get in front of a buyer. Registration alone rarely brings work. Find the teams that buy language services (localization, legal, HR, accessibility, patient services), attend supplier diversity events, and consider subcontracting to the large language vendors these companies already use. Many big companies track diverse spending by their main suppliers, which makes a diverse subcontractor attractive to those vendors.
  6. Track follow-ups here. Set a status and a follow-up date on each company.
